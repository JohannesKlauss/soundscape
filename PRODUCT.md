## Platform

desktop

macOS first (confirmed 2026-11-01). Windows and Linux remain aspirations for later — the released app shipped all three (.dmg, .msi, .AppImage/.deb) — but the rewrite designs and polishes for macOS now. Note: the impeccable platform taxonomy (web/ios/android/adaptive) has no desktop value; `desktop` is recorded deliberately, and no native-mobile reference applies to this gpui app.

## Users

Game masters running live tabletop RPG sessions. The defining situation is mid-session: the GM is narrating, tracking the table, and needs to trigger or crossfade atmosphere without the software demanding attention the table needs. Secondary, same person at a different time: the GM preparing scenes and moods ahead of a session.

## Product Purpose

Soundscape is a desktop soundboard for TTRPG sessions. It combines music, loops, ambient sounds, and one-shot effects into playable atmospheres that the GM triggers and crossfades between during play. It exists to make the table immersive without making the GM fight the tool. Success for the rewrite: a GM can operate it live, one-handed and glanceable, without breaking narration — the released app's UI was too clunky for that, which is why the rewrite exists.

## Positioning

The local-first soundboard built for live, in-session operation — not a prep tool you tolerate at the table. Offline by architecture, not by fallback: no account, no ads, no subscription, no bullshit. Where neighbors optimize for library management or streaming catalogs, Soundscape optimizes for the ten seconds in which the GM needs the tavern to become a cave.

## Operating Context

Two modes of use, same product:

- **Live at the table:** laptop next to the GM screen, attention on the players. Confirmed 2026-11-01: the app **lives in the background** during play and pops up for seconds at a time — the GM glances, changes the atmosphere, and is gone. The surface must be legible in a single glance, never reward staring. Interactions must survive distraction, low light, and one-handed operation. Misfires (wrong sound, abrupt cutoff) are felt by the whole table.
- **Prep between sessions:** building scenes and moods, adjusting per-sound volume, panning, and fades, importing material.
- Environments may be offline (conventions, game stores, basements); local-first is a hard requirement, not a preference.

## Capabilities and Constraints

Confirmed domain model (rewrite, per owner 2026-11-01):

- A **Scene** is the top-level playable container: it contains moods and sounds.
- A **Mood** is a prepared mix: multiple sounds combined with per-sound fades, panning, and volume.
- **Sounds** must also be triggerable individually, outside any mood.
- Moods must also be runnable **on the fly** (ad-hoc, not only pre-prepared).

Carried over from the released app (feature parity is the floor):

- Audio import from local files, URLs, Freesound, and YouTube.
- Crossfading between moods; fade-in/out and playback-order control on sounds.

Technical facts:

- Pure Rust: gpui/gpui-kit + gpui-navigator (UI), rodio (audio), native_db/native_model (local persistence), yt-dlp (YouTube import). Replaces the previous Tauri/Node app.
- Everything persists locally; no backend, no account.
- **Imagery-first tiles (confirmed 2026-11-01):** every mood and one-shot carries evocative background artwork — generated by AI when the mood/sound is saved, overridable by the user's own image. Artwork is a legibility feature, not decoration: the GM recognizes the tile by its image faster than by reading its name. AI generation here is a local authoring convenience at prep time; playback never depends on a network.

Undecided / open:

- Whether the old app's "pad" concept survives into the new domain language or folds into sounds/moods.
- Freesound import in the old app required a user-supplied API key; whether that flow carries over unchanged.
- Timing and commitment for Windows/Linux.

## Product Principles

1. **Live-session ergonomics outranks everything.** Every interaction must work mid-narration: glanceable, forgiving, one-handed. If a design is clever but costs the GM attention, it's wrong.
2. **Local-first, offline always.** No account, no network dependency, no feature that dies without internet.
3. **Prepared and spontaneous are equal citizens.** Pre-built moods and on-the-fly triggering/mixing are both first-class paths, not a hierarchy.
4. **Parity is the floor, not the ceiling.** The rewrite must do everything the released app does — with a fundamentally more ergonomic UI, never fewer capabilities.
5. **macOS-first craft.** Design and polish for macOS now, without decisions that would block Windows/Linux later.
